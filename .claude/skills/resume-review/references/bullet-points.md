# Bullet Points — the highest-leverage part of a résumé

A résumé is a 30-second pitch. Bullets are where it's won or lost. "Your résumé is **not**
your job description." Every bullet must prove capability with concrete, technical,
quantified accomplishments. Source: r/EngineeringResumes wiki.

## What each bullet should do

1. **Highlight the technical work** you actually did.
2. **Highlight a technical challenge** you faced and overcame.
3. **Highlight the impact** of your work — ideally a number.

## General rules

- [ ] **1–2 lines long**, aim for **1 sentence** per bullet.
- [ ] **Ordered most relevant/impressive first** — reviewers may only read the first one.
- [ ] **No personal pronouns** (I, we, us, my, our, their).
- [ ] **No ending periods** — bullets aren't sentences.
- [ ] **Never extend further right than the dates.**
- [ ] **No orphan lines** — don't spill a bullet onto a new line holding only 1–4 words.
- [ ] **No mid-word hyphenation** from wrapping.
- [ ] **Avoid sub-bullets** — they clutter and add verbosity.
- [ ] **Avoid apostrophes `'`, ampersands `&`, slashes `/`.**
- [ ] **Few adjectives/adverbs** — they're superfluous (see banned filler below).
- [ ] **Digits, not words:** "8", not "eight".
- [ ] **Quantify, and move the metric toward the start** of the bullet when possible.

## Every bullet starts with a strong past-tense action verb

`Led` is the past tense of `lead`.

**Good action verbs:** analyzed, architected, automated, built, created, decreased,
designed, developed, implemented, improved, optimized, published, reduced, refactored.

**Weak/banned verbs — flag and replace:** aided, assisted, coded, collaborated,
communicated, executed, exposed to, gained experience, helped, participated, programmed,
ran, used, **utilized**, worked on.

> Never use **utilize** — 9/10 times it bloats a sentence into a Gordian knot.

**Superfluous / awkward / overcomplex verbs — avoid:** amplified, conceptualized, crafted,
elevated, employed, engaged, engineered, enhanced, ensured, fostered, headed, honed,
innovated, mastered, orchestrated, perfected, pioneered, revolutionized, spearheaded,
transformed.

**Frequently misused — handle with care:** leverage, enhance, utilize.

**Banned filler adjectives:** excellent, innovative, expert, revolutionary, disruptive.
**Banned filler adverbs:** creatively, diligently, meticulously, strategically,
successfully, independently, innovatively, excellently, expertly.

## Structure: XYZ / STAR / CAR

Every bullet should follow one of these. **XYZ is the easiest to apply.**

- **XYZ** — "Accomplished **[X]** as measured by **[Y]**, by doing **[Z]**."
- **STAR** — Situation, Task, Action, Result.
- **CAR** — Challenge, Action, Result.

If you can quantify the result, **move the metric toward the start** of the bullet.

## Prompts to generate bullet content

**What did I do?** Did I solve a problem? Team or solo? How many people on the team?

**How did I do it?** Why this approach? What tools/resources (frameworks, libraries,
standards)? What testing validated the design, and how did it change the outcome?

**What were the results?** Delivered on time / under budget? Did it work as intended? What
went wrong and what did I learn?

### Exceeded-the-goal exercise

Every project started with goals you had to exert effort to meet. You met them (don't list
failures). Now: **how** did you meet them, and did you **exceed** them — deliver in half the
time, double the throughput, cover more cases? Brain-dump it all freely first, then pare
down to fit space and the strongest content.

## Integration over parts lists

Don't throw a parts/tech list at the reader. It matters less *what* components you used and
more *how you integrated* them to accomplish the goal. Not "uses a Raspberry Pi and a
stepper motor" — instead, *how* the software interfaced with the motors to hit a precision
target. That shows technical ability.

## Handling sensitive / NDA content

Talk about the **technologies behind it** without the forbidden specifics. Choose words
carefully:

- Instead of "developed C++ software for testing LANTIRN display pod software on the F-14D",
  say "developed C++ software for evaluating aircraft subsystems on a [standard]-based test
  bench" or "created a proof-of-concept integrating COTS items into a data recorder."
- Instead of revealing proprietary carbon-fiber layups: "a new standard process to form
  composite parts, reducing lead times by 33%."

Still apply STAR to flesh it out. If they pry deeper in an interview, that's when you say
"no."

## Before / after rewrites

| Weak (job duty) | Strong (XYZ accomplishment) |
|---|---|
| Worked on payment integrations | Integrated 10+ payment providers and 70+ channels across Asia, expanding regional checkout coverage |
| Responsible for CI/CD setup | Introduced CI/CD and error tracking, improving release confidence and incident response |
| Utilized Laravel to build APIs | Architected backend APIs for a national learning platform serving 10,000+ schools |
| Helped migrate legacy apps | Refactored and migrated two legacy Laravel apps onto a maintainable, tested foundation |

Each "after" leads with a strong verb, adds context, and carries a measurable result.
