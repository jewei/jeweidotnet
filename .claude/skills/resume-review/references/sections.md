# Per-Section Rules

Section-by-section standards from the r/EngineeringResumes wiki, with the senior-engineer
(10+ YoE) and career-changer playbooks at the end.

## Work Experience

- [ ] **Name it `Work Experience` or `Experience`** — never "Professional Experience",
      "Selected Experience", "Employment History", "Employment", or "Job History".
- [ ] **Paid work only** (research may go here too). Flag unpaid items.
- [ ] **Label internships as internships** and contract roles as contract.
- [ ] **Order roles and bullets by relevance/impressiveness** — best stuff first.
- [ ] **Tailor to the job description** — show you can do *this* job.
- [ ] **Add context.** Listing positions + buzzwords isn't enough; say what you did and what
      problems you solved.
- [ ] **Differentiate, don't be humble.** Accomplishments, metrics, technical wins — not job
      duties. The résumé is not a job description.
- [ ] **Lead with engineering substance**, not the tool. The software is an "oh, by the way";
      the engineering judgment behind it is the differentiator.
- [ ] **Bullets, not paragraphs.** (See `bullet-points.md`.)

## Education

- [ ] **No coursework** unless extremely specialized/notable (e.g., Underwater Autonomous
      Robotics).
- [ ] **No high school.**
- [ ] **No schools without a completed degree.**
- [ ] **Graduation date only** — no start date, no range. Student: "Expected May 2025".
      Graduated: "May 2021" or just "2021".
- [ ] **Reverse chronological** — master's above bachelor's.
- [ ] **GPA:** include only if >3.75, 2 decimals max. **Remove it once you have a full-time
      job** unless truly exceptional. (This user: remove GPA.)
- [ ] **Format:** "Bachelor's of Science", "Master's of Science".
- [ ] **No school location** if it's in the name (MIT, UCLA) or commonly known.
- [ ] **No awards/scholarships** unless extraordinary (Rhodes, Fulbright).
- [ ] **D1/competitive sports** may be included.

## Skills

- [ ] **Name it `Skills`** — not "Technical Skills" or "Relevant Skills".
- **Purpose:** a few words on the technical skills you bring; ideally match the job
  description. Not a buzzword dump.
- [ ] **Include** languages you've used thoroughly and could interview in; frameworks/tools/
      programs you've actually used.
- [ ] **Repeat across bullets** — skills listed here should appear in your experience bullets
      and vice versa.
- [ ] **No soft skills** (teamwork, leadership) — demonstrate those in bullets.
- [ ] **No assumed/taken-for-granted skills** (typing, MS Word, "IDEs").
- [ ] **No code-host names** — use "Git" or "SVN", not GitHub/GitLab/Bitbucket.
- [ ] **No operating systems, IDEs, or editors** (VS Code, vim, LaTeX) as skills, generally.
- **Formatting:**
  - [ ] **≤3 lines, single column.** Listing skills vertically wastes space.
  - [ ] **Most important first**; question whether trailing items belong at all.
  - [ ] **Group into categories** (Languages, Technologies, Frameworks, Tools, etc.) — one
        line / one column per group.
  - [ ] **Comma-separated**, properly punctuated — not hyphens, dashes, or pipes.
  - [ ] **Capitalize proper names correctly** (SolidWorks, CATIA V5, LabVIEW).
  - [ ] **Split functionally different skills:** "C, C++", not "C/C++".
  - [ ] **No random bolding**, no "Expert in"/"Professional in" descriptors.
- [ ] **No hobbies here** — only in a separate subsection, and only if nothing better fits.
- **New to industry?** A few well-honed skills beat 10+ you've barely touched. Distinguish
  what you've *used in industry* from what you've merely *touched*.

## Projects

- [ ] **Name it `Projects`** — not Academic/Personal/Relevant/Selected/Technical Projects.
- **Scope:** personal projects, student design teams, hobby/extracurricular — **not** work
  projects.
- [ ] **No "project" in the title** (redundant); capitalize titles correctly.
- [ ] **Personal projects:** roles/locations/dates usually unnecessary; a link to the repo/
      portfolio matters more.
- [ ] **Don't label** "Personal/Academic/Group Project" beside the title.
- [ ] **Bullet points, not paragraphs**; order by relevance + impressiveness.
- **Make them count:** real projects with users (even just you) that are actively
  maintained — not mandatory school projects or tutorial clones.

## Portfolios

- Link GitHub/portfolio **only if up-to-date and substantive.** Empty repos or stale
  portfolios → omit and save space. These links are not obligatory.
- Publish only projects with **good READMEs**: summary, screenshots, run instructions, test
  instructions. Include runnable automated tests (unit/e2e).
- **No full URLs** — drop `https://www.`.

## Senior Engineers and above (10+ YoE) — applies to this user

- [ ] Include a **brief summary (≤2 sentences).**
- [ ] **Separate résumés for management vs. IC** positions.
- [ ] **Try not to exceed 2 pages.**
- [ ] Mention **"soft" achievements** on top of business results.
- [ ] Mention not just **impact** but also **influence** (mentoring, standards, architecture
      ownership, subject-matter expertise).
- [ ] **Make earlier roles more concise** — trim the oldest jobs to a line or two.
- [ ] **Move Education to the bottom.**

## Career changers

- [ ] Include a **2-sentence summary** explaining the change and motivation, so reviewers
      have context.
- [ ] **Link working projects and source code.**
- [ ] **Be concise** about past (unrelated) work experience.
- [ ] **Keep it to 1 page.**
